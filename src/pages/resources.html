Method

How to read a paper

We use a three-pass method, adapted from S. Keshav’s How to Read a Paper (2007).

  1. Pass one, 15 minutes. Title, abstract, introduction, figures, conclusion. Can you state the main claim in one sentence?
  2. Pass two, about an hour. Methods and results. Separate what the authors claim from what their experiments show, and list the assumptions.
  3. Pass three, only for papers you’ll build on. Reproduce one figure or result in code.

Bring three things to every discussion: one point you’d defend, one you doubt, and one question.

4 papers

Concrete Problems in AI Safety

Amodei et al.2016Week 4

What it showsFrames five practical research problems (side effects, reward hacking, scalable supervision, safe exploration, distributional shift) with simple machine-learning examples.

How to read itRead sections 1 and 2 and the one problem that interests you most. Ask which of the five has seen real progress since 2016.

The Alignment Problem from a Deep Learning Perspective

Ngo, Chan and Mindermann2022Week 4

What it showsArgues how models trained with today’s methods could come to pursue goals that conflict with their developers’ intentions.

How to read itRead in full. Mark each claim as supported by evidence or argued from theory.

Unsolved Problems in ML Safety

Hendrycks, Carlini, Schulman and Steinhardt2021

What it showsMaps safety research into robustness, monitoring, alignment and systemic safety.

How to read itSkim it as a map of the field, then go deep on one area.

International AI Safety Report 2026

Bengio (chair) et al.2026Week 10

What it showsThe second edition of the government-backed synthesis of evidence on the capabilities, risks and safeguards of general-purpose AI, written by over 100 experts.

How to read itRead the executive summary first, then the chapter on risk management.

7 papers

Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences

Christiano et al.2017Week 5

What it showsAn agent can learn complex behaviour from a reward model trained on human comparisons of short clips, with feedback on a small fraction of its experience.

How to read itFocus on the reward-model setup. Ask what happens if raters can be fooled.

Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback

Ouyang et al.2022Week 5

What it showsThe InstructGPT pipeline: supervised fine-tuning, a reward model, then RLHF. Raters preferred a much smaller fine-tuned model over the original GPT-3.

How to read itRead the methods and the main results figures. Note whose preferences are being optimised.

Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback

Bai et al.2022Week 5

What it showsTrains a harmless assistant from a written set of principles and AI-generated feedback in place of human harm labels.

How to read itCompare it step by step with InstructGPT. Ask who writes the constitution.

Goal Misgeneralization in Deep Reinforcement Learning

Langosco et al.2022Week 4

What it showsAgents can keep their skills out of distribution while pursuing the wrong goal, such as heading for the end of a level instead of the coin.

How to read itRead the introduction and the CoinRun experiment. Ask how you’d detect this before deployment.

Weak-to-Strong Generalization

Burns et al.2023Week 6

What it showsTests whether a weak model’s labels can draw out a stronger model’s abilities, as an analogy for humans supervising more capable systems.

How to read itUnderstand the “performance gap recovered” metric. Ask where the analogy breaks.

Sleeper Agents

Hubinger et al.2024Week 6

What it showsDeliberately backdoored behaviour in language models can survive standard safety training.

How to read itSeparate what is shown (an inserted behaviour persists) from what is not (that such behaviour arises on its own).

AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion

Greenblatt et al.2023Week 6

What it showsEvaluates protocols for using a possibly untrustworthy model safely, with a weaker trusted model and limited human review.

How to read itFocus on the red-team and blue-team setup. Ask which assumptions make the problem tractable.

Interpretability

6 papers

Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits

Olah et al.2020Week 7

What it showsArgues that networks can be understood as features connected into circuits, with evidence from vision models.

How to read itRead in full. List its three central claims and the evidence offered for each.

A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits

Elhage et al.2021Week 7

What it showsDecomposes small attention-only transformers into interpretable paths and introduces the residual stream view.

How to read itWork through it with a notebook open. Reread the residual stream section until you can explain it aloud.

In-context Learning and Induction Heads

Olsson et al.2022Week 7

What it showsPresents evidence that induction heads drive much of in-context learning and form during a sudden phase change in training.

How to read itRate the strength of each line of evidence separately.

Interpretability in the Wild

Wang et al.2022Week 8

What it showsReverse-engineers the circuit GPT-2 small uses to complete sentences such as “Mary and John went to the store; John gave a drink to …”

How to read itFollow the activation-patching figures. Note the faithfulness, completeness and minimality tests.

Toy Models of Superposition

Elhage et al.2022Week 9

What it showsSmall networks store more features than they have dimensions when features are sparse, which explains polysemantic neurons.

How to read itRead the introduction and key results, then run the accompanying notebook.

Towards Monosemanticity

Bricken et al.2023Week 9

What it showsSparse autoencoders extract features from a small transformer that are far more interpretable than its neurons.

How to read itBrowse the interactive feature pages. Ask how you would know a feature’s label is correct.

Evaluations and governance

3 papers

Model Evaluation for Extreme Risks

Shevlane et al.2023Week 10

What it showsArgues for evaluating dangerous capabilities and alignment, and for feeding the results into training, deployment and security decisions.

How to read itRead the framework section. Ask who should run evaluations, and who checks them.

Frontier AI Regulation

Anderljung et al.2023

What it showsProposes building blocks for regulating the most capable models: standards, registration and reporting, and compliance mechanisms.

How to read itCompare its proposals with what governments have done since. Ask which could apply in Iraq.

Computing Power and the Governance of Artificial Intelligence

Sastry et al.2024Week 10

What it showsArgues compute is unusually governable, since it is detectable, excludable, quantifiable and concentrated, and weighs the policy options.

How to read itRead the summary and one policy section. Ask what compute governance means for countries that import all their hardware.

Study paths

Four routes through this library

For self-learners. Each path has a time estimate and a clear finish line.

Path 0 · 6 to 8 weeks · about 5 h a week

Get ready for the program

  1. Python until you can write classes and use NumPy arrays without loops.
  2. 3Blue1Brown’s Essence of Linear Algebra and Neural Networks video series.
  3. Neural Networks: Zero to Hero, lectures 1 and 2.

Done when you pass the self-check on the program page.

Path A · 12 to 16 weeks · about 10 h a week

Technical alignment

  1. ARENA Chapter 0 (fundamentals), then the first sections of Chapter 1 (transformers).
  2. The Start here and Alignment papers above, in order.
  3. BlueDot’s Technical AI Safety course alongside, for discussion.

Done when you’ve replicated one result from a paper in this library, with a public repository and a two-page write-up.

Path B · 10 to 12 weeks · about 8 h a week

Mechanistic interpretability

  1. Karpathy’s Let’s build GPT lecture.
  2. Neel Nanda’s Concrete Steps guide.
  3. ARENA Chapter 1 sections on TransformerLens, induction heads, indirect object identification, superposition and sparse autoencoders.
  4. The interpretability papers above, in order.

Done when you can present a behaviour in GPT-2 small and the patching evidence for the circuit behind it.

Path C · 8 to 10 weeks · about 5 h a week

AI governance with technical literacy

  1. BlueDot’s AGI Strategy course, then its Frontier AI Governance course.
  2. The governance chapters of Introduction to AI Safety, Ethics and Society.
  3. The International AI Safety Report 2026, then the evaluations and governance papers above.

Done when you’ve written a two-page brief on one AI governance question facing Iraq or the Kurdistan Region.
