Overview

The program is an introduction to AI safety through conversation. Each week a small group meets with a facilitator to talk through one question: how today’s AI works, what can go wrong, and what people around the world are doing about it.

The first six weeks introduce AI safety. The last six turn to you: how to communicate, work with others, present yourself, and plan a successful career in AI or in your own field. There is no coding and nothing to read before a session. You only need to come ready to think and talk.

Who it’s for

  • University students in any field, at any university in the Kurdistan Region or Iraq, undergraduate or postgraduate.
  • Recent graduates and young professionals who want to understand how AI is changing their work and their options.
  • Anyone curious about AI and its risks who wants to take part in the conversation, whatever their background.

No prior knowledge of AI or AI safety is expected.

What you need

  • CuriosityAn interest in how AI is changing the world and a willingness to share your view and listen to others.
  • EnglishEnough to follow and join a discussion. You don’t need perfect English.
  • TimeOne session a week for 12 weeks.
  • Age18 or over on the start date.

Not needed: programming, mathematics, a technical degree, or any background in AI.

Week0

Welcome meeting

One week before the start ({{week0_date}}). Meet your facilitators and the rest of the cohort, agree how the group will work together, and share what you hope to get from the 12 weeks.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–3

AI and AI safety

By the end you can explain in plain words how today’s AI is made, and what people mean by AI safety.

Week1

What is AI today?

Topics
How modern AI systems such as chatbots are made; what they do well and where they fail; where we already meet AI in daily life, study and work.
Discussion
Where did you use AI this week, and what did you trust it to do?

Week2

What is AI safety?

Topics
The idea that AI systems may not do what we intend; a short history of the field; the communities and organisations working on it today.
Discussion
What would it mean for an AI system to be safe, and safe for whom?

Week3

When AI gets it wrong

Topics
Real examples of AI systems behaving in ways their makers didn’t intend: mistakes stated with confidence, bias, gaming their goals, and misuse; why these problems are hard to fix.
Discussion
Which example worries you most, and why?

Phase 2 · Weeks 4–6

Risks and responses

By the end you can weigh the main arguments about AI risk, and describe how different people work to reduce it.

Week4

Risks from more capable AI

Topics
The arguments for and against concern about more powerful future AI systems; how experts disagree, and what they agree on.
Discussion
What is the strongest argument against worrying, and does it convince you?

Week5

How people work on AI safety

Topics
The main kinds of work: technical research, testing and evaluating AI systems, policy and governance, and communication; the people who do each, and the skills they use.
Discussion
Which kind of work fits your strengths and interests best?

Week6

AI, society and our region

Topics
AI and jobs, education, misinformation and law; what AI means for Iraq and the Kurdistan Region; AI and the Kurdish language.
Discussion
Which decisions about AI will be made here, and where can people in this region have real influence?

Phase 3 · Weeks 7–9

Becoming a professional

By the end you can present your ideas and yourself with confidence.

Week7

Communicating clearly

Topics
Explaining a complex idea simply, in writing and out loud; structuring a short talk; answering questions you didn’t expect.
In the session
Each participant explains one idea from the program in three minutes, and gets friendly feedback.

Week8

Working with others

Topics
Teamwork and leadership; giving and receiving feedback; professional emails and messages; running and taking part in meetings.
Discussion
Think of the best team you’ve been part of. What made it work?

Week9

Your professional profile

Topics
Writing a clear CV; a LinkedIn profile that works for you; presenting your experience honestly, including volunteering and student projects.
In the session
Review each other’s CVs in pairs, with a checklist from the facilitators.

Phase 4 · Weeks 10–12

Building your career

By the end you have a personal plan for your next six months.

Week10

Paths into AI and AI safety

Topics
Study at universities in the region and abroad, scholarships, fellowships and online programs, jobs and internships; how people in the field got their start.
Discussion
Which path fits where you are now, and what would the first step be?

Week11

Applications and interviews

Topics
Writing a strong application and motivation letter; preparing for interviews; following up after a rejection.
In the session
Practice interviews in pairs, with feedback.

Week12

Your next step

Session
Each participant shares a personal plan for the next six months: a program to apply to, a skill to build, or a person to learn from.
Closing
Certificates of completion, and how to stay involved with the Hub.

During the 12 weeks we may hold special events, such as a talk by someone working in AI or AI safety, a visit, or an evening with the wider community. Events are an extra, not a requirement, and we announce each one in advance on News and events.

  1. Submit the application form

    About 20 minutes, by {{apply_deadline}} at 23:59 Erbil time. You also mark the hours in the week when you could attend.

  2. Talk with a facilitator

    A 20-minute conversation, online or in Erbil, between {{interviews_from}} and {{interviews_to}}. We talk about what you hope to get from the program and your availability. No trick questions.

  3. Hear our decision

    By {{decisions_date}}. Confirm your place within seven days and join the welcome meeting before {{cohort_start}}.

What we look for

  • Curiosity about AI and its effects on people.
  • Clear thinking in your written answers, in your own words.
  • Real interest in the questions, not only the certificate.
  • Realistic availability for all 12 weeks.
  • Someone who makes the group better: asks questions, helps others, disagrees respectfully.

What we don’t require

A particular university, field of study or grade average, any coding or technical background, prior knowledge of AI safety, or perfect English.

What we expect once you’re in

Come to every session you can, ready to take part. Tell your facilitator early if you will miss a week, and follow the Hub’s code of conduct.

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost anything?

No. The program is free.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. The program has no coding and no technical exercises. It is built on discussion.

Do I get university credit?

No. You receive a certificate of completion from the Hub.

Can I do the whole program online?

Yes. The online section follows the same weekly topics.

I don’t live in the Kurdistan Region. Can I apply?

Yes. Priority goes to applicants living in the Kurdistan Region and the rest of Iraq; Kurdish diaspora and other applicants are considered if places remain.

Is there an age requirement?

You must be 18 or over on the start date.

What if I miss a session?

You can miss up to two. Tell your facilitator in advance.

How is the session time chosen?

Everyone marks the hours they’re free on the application form, and we choose the time that works for most of the cohort.

Will there be a Cohort 2?

We intend to run the program again after reviewing Cohort 1. Join the mailing list below to hear first.

Who leads the sessions?

Trained facilitators from the Hub’s team, listed on the About page.
